# Case: Website Redesign Animation

Source: the user's supplied brief in this conversation. This file is a structured production version of that brief, not evidence of an actual client project.

## Objective

A client asks Daan to improve a website. The viewer enters the linked old website, watches its recognizable components reorganize into the new design, explores the finished desktop and mobile experience, sees a matched before/after comparison, and returns to the original conversation. The finished website is the advertisement.

**Duration:** 35 seconds.

**Aspect ratio:** not specified in the original brief. Choose explicitly for production.

**Language:** preserve the supplied English copy unless the user requests localization.

**Narration:** not requested. Default to no voiceover.

## Exact visible copy

Opening incoming message:

> Hey Daan, could you improve our website?

Place the actual chosen website link beneath the message. Until supplied, treat it as an unresolved input; do not impersonate a real customer.

Opening reply:

> Of course!

Returning message:

> Could you improve our website?

Returning reply:

> Of course.

Final card:

> Ready for a better website? Send us your link.

Brand: subtle WebsUp branding, using a supplied/verified logo or a clearly temporary text treatment. Do not invent an official logo or palette.

## Master timeline

Intervals are start-inclusive and end-exclusive, except the final endpoint.

| Beat | Time | Visible action | Continuity and sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 — Request | 0.00–3.00 | Incoming message and website link. Large, readable chat treatment. | Subtle bubble settlement; one notification. No opening brand bumper. |
| 02 — Reply | 3.00–4.50 | “Of course!” appears; cursor approaches and clicks the link. | Send sound and distinct but restrained click. Begin building musical energy. |
| 03 — Enter website | 4.50–6.00 | Link expands into a browser showing the old site. | Expand from the link's actual on-screen position; conversation recedes behind it. |
| 04 — Establish before | 6.00–10.00 | Show the old hero, then a legible portion of the page with a controlled scroll. | Straight-on and authentic. Do not artificially degrade the original. |
| 05 — Begin rebuild | 10.00–12.00 | Existing sections separate; spacing opens; brief guides clarify the new structure. | Maintain mapped identities and visible scroll/camera continuity. |
| 06 — Build new design | 12.00–15.00 | Heading, image, navigation, CTA and supporting content reorganize in a deliberate sequence. | Land major changes in rhythm. Do not move everything simultaneously. |
| 07 — Final detail | 15.00–16.00 | Final button settles, cursor triggers its hover state, guides leave. | One satisfying detail sound; brief reduction before reveal. |
| 08 — Hero reveal | 16.00–20.00 | Finished homepage hero at readable scale. | Hold and let the design breathe; only a subtle push-in. Strong musical landing. |
| 09 — Explore | 20.00–24.00 | Scroll to one or two standout sections; demonstrate one purposeful interaction. | Follow the content, not a disconnected camera montage. |
| 10 — Mobile | 24.00–27.00 | Browser narrows, content reflows, mobile menu opens and closes. | Same site and shared elements. Keep menu dwell readable; no separate phone mockup scene. |
| 11 — Comparison | 27.00–30.00 | Return to desktop hero; show old and new in matching framing with a comparison divider. End on new. | Identical viewport, camera, scroll position and clipping for both endpoints. |
| 12 — Return | 30.00–32.00 | Browser contracts to the original chat; question returns, followed by “Of course.” | Reverse the link expansion geometrically. Familiar message cue. |
| 13 — End card | 32.00–35.00 | Subtle WebsUp brand and final CTA. | Stable text, clean music resolution, no distracting final animation. |

## Pacing decisions

The old website gets a genuine reference window. The 6-second rebuild should not consume the 8-second finished-desktop presentation or the mobile/comparison beats. Holds that allow reading are intentional, not empty time to fill with decorative movement.

The mobile beat is deliberately short: show one layout transformation and one menu interaction, not an additional tour. The comparison beat must include the visible return to matching desktop framing; do not consume it entirely with device motion.

## Portrait composition note

If making a 9:16 cut, show the desktop overview as context, then frame the important content large enough to understand. Do not claim that every navigation item on a wide desktop page is readable when the entire page is reduced to a narrow social frame. Recompose close-ups and camera focus rather than cropping away the story.
